Method and back-test

How the outlook is made

  1. Take the latest value and date published on each NASA indicator page; never relabel older data as "October 2026".
  2. Fit the recent trend (e.g. CO₂ rose 2.61 ppm/yr on average in 2020–25).
  3. Adjust for ENSO, the largest source of year-to-year swings: El Niño weakens land carbon sinks and warms the surface.
  4. Give a range, not a single number, and widen it where data are sparse or annual.
  5. Back-test: forecast past years without looking at them, then compare with what happened.

Back-test: CO₂ annual mean, 2021–2025

YearObservedTrend-only errorTrend + ENSO error

Trend-only = previous year + mean rise of the five years before. Trend + ENSO = Met Office forecasts issued ahead of each year. Adding ENSO cut the error most in the El Niño year 2024.
